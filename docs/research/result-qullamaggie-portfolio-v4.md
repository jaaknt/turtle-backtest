## Findings & Ideas to Improve Portfolio Performance

**The limit-order entry (buy 3% below the signal-day close, resting 30 calendar days) is a clear, broad improvement for the two weaker cohorts, and a mixed but often better one for `s20_tr20`.** For `s15_tr20`, LIMIT-3% beats EOD on *both* Calmar and Final$ at every size except a near-tie at 3% Sortino — e.g. at 6% size, Calmar goes 1.165→1.487 and Final$ goes $180,981→$248,335 (+37%). For `s12_tr20`, LIMIT-3% wins outright at every single size on both metrics — at 8% size, Calmar goes 0.787→0.989 and Final$ goes $178,948→$233,035 (+30%). For `s20_tr20` the pattern flips with size: EOD wins Calmar at 3-5% (its best being 2.111 vs LIMIT's 1.610 at 3%), but LIMIT wins at 6-8%, and LIMIT@8% produces the single best Final$ across the entire 36-combination sweep ($377,029 vs EOD@8%'s $258,512, +46%).

**Why the limit order helps more as position size grows**: paying 3% less for the same eventual winner directly compounds into a larger per-trade return, and that effect matters more in dollar/Calmar terms the bigger each position is. At small sizes the cost is the selection/delay effect — waiting up to 30 days for a pullback means missing the (usually shorter and stronger) immediate continuations that `s20_tr20`'s tightest signals are most likely to produce, which is why EOD still wins Calmar for `s20_tr20` at 3-5% specifically. The two effects roughly offset at 5-6% and the discount advantage dominates from there.

**This reframes the earlier "no size dominates" finding**: once the limit-order alternative is on the table, `s20_tr20`'s "optimal size" question splits into two separate answers depending on entry method — 3% if buying at the close, 8% if using a 3%-discount limit order — rather than one universal answer for the cohort.

**Sample sizes are similar between EOD and LIMIT-3%** (e.g. `s20_tr20`@3%: 160 EOD trades vs. 154 LIMIT trades) since almost every signal in this bull-leaning 2020-2026 window eventually retraces 3% within 30 days — so this comparison isn't confounded by one approach having drastically fewer observations than the other, unlike the earlier next-day-only limit-order sensitivity tests in this research thread.

**This 2020-2026 window still inflates every metric relative to the full 2010-2026 history** (as found in the prior run) — treat the absolute Calmar/Sortino/Final$ values here, for both entry approaches, as a favorable-regime ceiling rather than a baseline expectation.

**Two previously-identified untested levers remain the most promising next steps, and now interact with the limit-order finding**: (1) an early-exit rule (e.g. "3 consecutive closes below 200d SMA") would still help most where MaxDD balloons, which under LIMIT-3% is now `s20_tr20`@8% (-38.70%) instead of the EOD equivalent; (2) rank-based funding (`RANK_FUNDING = False`) matters even more under the limit-order approach, since a resting order sitting for up to 30 days creates more opportunities for multiple pending orders to compete for the same day's freed-up cash than a same-day EOD fill does.
