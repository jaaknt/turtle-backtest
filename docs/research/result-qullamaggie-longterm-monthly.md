## Findings

### 1. Stricter SMA threshold wins on every aggregate metric — the strongest confirmation of this pattern all session

| | s12 (12%) | s15 (15%) | s17 (17%) | s20 (20%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| N (19yr total) | 3656 | 2762 | 2250 | 1577 |
| Win% | 68.9% | 70.3% | 70.3% | 73.0% |
| Mean% | +34.84% | +38.77% | +40.91% | +48.54% |
| Med% | +22.06% | +25.19% | +25.90% | +31.97% |
| Sortino | 1.064 | 1.200 | 1.261 | 1.466 |
| CVaR95% | -60.31% | -58.73% | -59.39% | -59.45% |

Win%, Mean%, Med%, and Sortino all increase monotonically from s12 → s20, while CVaR95% (tail risk) stays essentially flat at ~-59 to -60% regardless of threshold — the extra return from a stricter breakout filter isn't bought with proportionally worse downside. This is a materially stronger result than either single-window test run earlier this session: the [2021-2026 backtest](result-qullamaggie-backtest-v4.md) showed the same s20-best ordering, but the [2010-2015 out-of-sample run](result-qullamaggie-backtest-v4-2010-2015.md) showed it reversing (s15/s17 beating s20). Aggregated over 19 years and ~15x more trades than either shorter window, the ordering resolves decisively in favor of stricter thresholds.

### 2. But the ordering is not reliable year-by-year — it inverts in choppy/correction years

| Year | Regime | s12 Mean% | s15 Mean% | s17 Mean% | s20 Mean% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | correction | -15.4 | -15.6 | **-19.9** | -16.5 |
| 2018 | correction | +8.2 | +12.1 | +10.8 | **-2.8** |
| 2019 | choppy | +21.5 | +23.4 | +15.7 | +15.5 |
| 2022 | bear | +5.8 | +6.2 | +3.9 | **+3.1** |

In every one of these four weaker years, the *loosest* threshold (s12) beats or ties the *strictest* (s20) — 2018 is the sharpest case, where s20 goes outright negative (-2.8%) while s12 stays solidly positive (+8.2%). By contrast, the aggregate advantage for s20 is concentrated in strong trending years (2009, 2012-2013, 2016-2017, 2020, 2023-2025), where the most-extended breakouts (20%+ above SMA50) keep extending. In choppier markets those same extended names appear more prone to failed-breakout whipsaws. **The "raise the ranking bar" conclusion from earlier this session is real in aggregate, but is a bull-market effect, not a universal one** — small per-year N (as low as 2 trades in 2008 for s17/s20) makes some of this noisy, but the direction is consistent across four separate weak years, not a single outlier.

### 3. Frequency vs. conviction trade-off

s12 fires ~2.3x more often than s20 (3656 vs 1577 trades over 19 years, roughly 192/yr vs 83/yr). s12/s15 keep more capital deployed more of the time at a lower per-trade edge; s20 is far more selective with a larger edge per trade when it fires, but risks sitting in cash during quieter stretches (e.g. only 2 s20 signals in all of 2008 and 2011 combined).

### 4. Returns are strongly right-skewed for every threshold

Mean% sits well above Med% across the board (e.g. s12 ALL: +34.8% mean vs +22.1% median) — a handful of huge winners pull the mean up. February 2020 is the extreme case: monthly average returns of +285% to +447% across the four configs (COVID-crash-into-recovery breakouts with a 366-day hold landing in early 2021's melt-up). Typical/median outcomes are meaningfully more modest than the headline Mean% suggests for all four strategies.

### 5. Regime concentration

Trade count tracks the `SPY>200d SMA` bull-regime filter tightly: 2020 and 2023-2025 alone account for 45.4% of all s12 trades, rising to 47.9% (s15), 49.4% (s17), and 53.7% (s20) — the stricter the threshold, the more concentrated in these four bull years its trades become. The five bear/correction years (2008, 2011, 2015, 2018, 2022) contribute a small fraction of total N by comparison. The 19-year average return is disproportionately a story of a few strong bull stretches, not a steady year-in-year-out edge — the same regime-concentration caveat noted in `qullamaggie-backtest-v4.md`'s findings, more visibly demonstrated here with a much longer sample.

## Pros / Cons by threshold

| | s12 | s15 | s17 | s20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pros** | Highest trade frequency (best capital deployment); most robust in choppy/correction years (2015/2018/2019/2022 all show s12 at or near the top) | Good balance of frequency and edge; second-best Sortino | Similar profile to s15, marginal edge, marginal frequency loss | Best aggregate Win%/Mean%/Med%/Sortino by a clear margin; same tail risk (CVaR) as looser thresholds |
| **Cons** | Lowest aggregate Sortino/Mean%/Med% of the four | Still meaningfully behind s20 on every aggregate metric | Least distinct — doesn't clearly beat s15 by enough to justify losing ~250 trades vs s15 | Worst choppy-year behavior (outright negative in 2018); fewest trades (83/yr) risks idle capital between bull runs |

**Practical read**: s20 is the best single-strategy choice for pure long-run expectancy, and its downside (CVaR) isn't worse than the looser thresholds — but its edge is concentrated in strong trending markets and it goes cold (or negative) in exactly the regimes where a trader might most want a working strategy. s12/s15 sacrifice some aggregate edge for materially better behavior when the market isn't trending. A ranking-weighted blend across all four bucket outputs (already partly what `QullamaggieRanking` does by weighting `pct_vs_sma50` at 50/100) is a reasonable middle ground rather than committing to one fixed threshold.

## Caveats

- **Survivorship bias is worse here than in the 5-year `qullamaggie-backtest-v4.py` window.** `company.market_cap` is a single current-day (2026) snapshot applied retroactively across all 19 years; the qualifying universe also excludes any ticker that isn't `status='active'` today. The 2008 GFC alone delisted or bankrupted many companies that would have qualified for signals at the time — those trades are structurally invisible to this backtest, and the effect compounds over 19 years far more than over 5.
- **Pre-2009 years are too thin to trust statistically.** 2007 (14-74 trades depending on strategy) and especially 2008 (2-7 trades) don't meet a reasonable minimum-sample bar for the printed Sortino/CVaR to mean much — treat those two years as directional color, not a reliable read on 2008-crisis performance.
- **No execution costs** — slippage, spread, and commissions are not modeled, same limitation as `qullamaggie-backtest-v4.py`.
- **close/high/low are split/dividend-adjusted** (scaled by `adjusted_close/close`); `raw_close` is used only for the `$5-$250` price-range filter, the real tradeable price at entry — same convention as `qullamaggie-backtest-v4.py`. This fix matters more here than in the 5-year backtest given far more split events occur across 19 years.
